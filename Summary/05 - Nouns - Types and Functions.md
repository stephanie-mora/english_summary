# Nouns - Types and Functions

## What is a Noun?

A **noun** is a word that represents a **person, place, thing, or idea**. We can think of nouns as "naming words" — everything we can see or talk about is represented by a word, and that word is a noun.

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **People** | soldier, Alan, cousin, lawyer |
| **Places** | house, London, factory, shelter |
| **Things (Objects)** | table, London Bridge, chisel, nitrogen |
| **Things (Animals)** | aardvark, rat, shark, Mickey |
| **Things (Ideas)** | confusion, kindness, faith, joy |

## Common Nouns and Proper Nouns

### Common Nouns

Common nouns are the **generic** words for a class of people, places, or things.

- boy, city, dog

### Proper Nouns

Proper nouns are the **names** given to people, places, or things to make them specific or unique. They always start with a **capital letter**.

- Michael, New York, Rover

### Comparison Table

| Common Noun | Proper Noun |
|-------------|-------------|
| boy | David |
| sailor | Adam |
| lawyer | Sarah |
| city | Boston |
| bridge | The Golden Gate Bridge |
| tower | Eiffel Tower |
| street | Honeysuckle Crescent |
| cat | Fido |

> **How to spot them:**
> - A common noun answers: *"What is it?"* → It is a **bridge**.
> - A proper noun answers: *"What's its name?"* → It is **London Bridge**.

## Types of Nouns

### Abstract Nouns

Things you **cannot** see or touch.

- bravery, joy, determination

### Concrete Nouns

Things you **can** see or touch.

- tree, hammer, cloud

### Collective Nouns

Words that denote **groups**.

- team, choir, pack

> **Key point:** Collective nouns can be treated as singular or plural depending on the sense of the sentence.
> - The team **is** scheduled to arrive at 4 o'clock. (as a unit)
> - The team **are** wearing different novelty hats. (as individuals)

### Compound Nouns

Nouns made up of **more than one word**.

- court-martial, pickpocket, water bottle

> **Two key points:**
> (1) Some are two words (*peace pipe*), some hyphenated (*play-off*), some single words (*eyeopener*).
> (2) Plurals add "s" to the **principal word**: *brothers-in-law*.

### Countable and Non-countable Nouns

| Countable | Non-countable |
|-----------|---------------|
| mountain / mountains | food (always singular) |
| fight / fights | music (always singular) |
| kiss / kisses | water (always singular) |

### Gender-specific Nouns

Nouns that are definitely male or female.

- king, vixen, actress

### Gerunds

Nouns that end in **-ing** and represent actions. They have **verb-like properties**.

- happily **building** a tower
- quickly **drawing** the scene
- suddenly **attacking** the enemy

### Verbal Nouns

Nouns derived from verbs. They have **no verb-like properties**.

- a good **building**
- a fine **drawing**
- an effective **attack**

> **Difference:** Gerunds are modified by adverbs; verbal nouns are modified by adjectives.

## The Function of Nouns

A noun can function as a **subject**, an **object**, or a **complement** within a sentence.

### Noun as Subject

- **Oranges** contain Vitamin C.
- **Ghosts** roam the corridors.

### Noun as Object

- He likes **oranges**.
- Cameras recorded the **ghosts**.

### Noun as Complement

- They were **oranges**.
- Those wailing sounds are **ghosts**.

## Attributive Nouns

A noun can be used like an **adjective** to modify another noun.

- oxygen tank
- diamond ring
- car door

In these examples, "oxygen," "diamond," and "car" are **attributive nouns**.

## Noun Phrases

A **noun phrase** is a noun with any sort of modifier (even "a" or "the").

- **Person:** the soldier, my cousin, dopey Alan
- **Place:** the house in the corner, inner London
- **Thing:** this table, the sharp chisel

A noun phrase can function as a **subject**, **object**, or **complement**:

- **Singing in the bath** relaxes me. (subject)
- I know **the back streets**. (object)
- She was **the devil in disguise**. (complement)

> **Test:** A noun phrase can be replaced by a pronoun.
> - **It** relaxes me.
> - I know **them**.
> - She was **him**.

## Noun Clauses

A **noun clause** functions as a noun in a sentence. It has a **subject** and a **verb**.

- I know **that the story is true**.
- I saw **how the accident happened**.
- I understand **why it was necessary**.
- I know **who said that**.

A noun clause can function as a **subject**, **object**, or **complement**:

- **Whoever smelt it** dealt it. (subject)
- I will give **what you said** some thought. (indirect object)
- My command is **whatever you wish**. (complement)

> **Test:** A noun clause can be replaced by a pronoun.
> - **It** is remarkable.

## A Great Test for Nouns

> A noun is any word or group of words that could be **replaced with a pronoun**.

This works for single words, phrases, and clauses.

## Key Points to Remember

1. A **noun** names a person, place, thing, or idea.
2. **Common nouns** are generic; **proper nouns** are specific and capitalized.
3. **Abstract** = cannot see/touch; **Concrete** = can see/touch.
4. **Collective nouns** can be singular or plural.
5. **Compound nouns** are made of multiple words.
6. **Countable** nouns have plurals; **non-countable** nouns don't.
7. **Gerunds** (-ing) have verb-like properties; **verbal nouns** don't.
8. Nouns can function as **subject**, **object**, or **complement**.
9. **Attributive nouns** modify other nouns like adjectives.
10. **Noun phrases** and **noun clauses** function as nouns.
11. **Test:** If you can replace it with a pronoun, it's a noun.
