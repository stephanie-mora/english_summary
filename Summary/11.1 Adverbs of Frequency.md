
# Adverbs of Frequency

We use **adverbs of frequency** to describe **how frequently** we do an activity.

## Common Adverbs of Frequency

| Frequency | Adverb | Example Sentence |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| 100% | **always** | I **always** go to bed before 11 p.m. |
| 90% | **usually** | I **usually** have cereal for breakfast. |
| 80% | **normally / generally** | I **normally** go to the gym. |
| 70% | **often / frequently** | I **often** surf the internet. |
| 50% | **sometimes** | I **sometimes** forget my wife's birthday. |
| 30% | **occasionally** | I **occasionally** eat junk food. |
| 10% | **seldom** | I **seldom** read the newspaper. |
| 5% | **hardly ever / rarely** | I **hardly ever** drink alcohol. |
| 0% | **never** | I **never** swim in the sea. |

> **Note:** These are also known as **Adverbs of INDEFINITE Frequency** because the exact frequency is not defined.

## Position of the Adverb in a Sentence

### 1. Before a Main Verb (except To Be)

> **Structure:** **Subject + adverb + main verb**

| Example |
|---------|
| I **always remember** to do my homework. |
| He **normally gets** good marks in exams. |
| She **often visits** her grandmother. |
| They **sometimes play** tennis on Saturdays. |

### 2. After the Verb To Be

> **Structure:** **Subject + to be + adverb**

| Example |
|---------|
| They **are never** pleased to see me. |
| She **isn't usually** bad tempered. |
| I **am always** happy to help. |
| He **is often** late for class. |

### 3. With an Auxiliary Verb

When we use an auxiliary verb (have, will, must, might, could, would, can, etc.), the adverb is placed **between the auxiliary and the main verb**. This is also true for **to be**.

> **Structure:** **Subject + auxiliary + adverb + main verb**

| Example |
|---------|
| She **can sometimes beat** me in a race. |
| I **would hardly ever be** unkind to someone. |
| They **might never see** each other again. |
| They **could occasionally be** heard laughing. |

## Adverbs at the Start of a Sentence

We **can** use the following adverbs at the **start** of a sentence:

- **Usually, normally, often, frequently, sometimes, occasionally**

**Examples:**
- **Occasionally**, I like to eat Thai food.
- **Sometimes**, I forget my keys.
- **Usually**, she arrives on time.

> **BUT** we **cannot** use the following at the beginning of a sentence:
> - **Always, seldom, rarely, hardly ever, never**
>
> ❌ *Always I go to bed early.*
> ✅ *I **always** go to bed early.*

## Hardly Ever and Never

We use **hardly ever** and **never** with **positive** verbs (not negative verbs).

| ✅ Correct | ❌ Incorrect |
|------------|--------------|
| She **hardly ever comes** to my parties. | *She doesn't hardly ever come...* |
| They **never say** 'thank you'. | *They don't never say...* |

## Ever in Questions and Negative Statements

We use **ever** in questions and negative statements.

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Question** | Have you **ever** been to New Zealand? |
| **Negative** | I haven't **ever** been to Switzerland. (= I have **never** been to Switzerland.) |


## Adverbs of Definite Frequency

We can also use these expressions when we want to be **more specific** about the frequency:

| Expression | Example |
|------------|---------|
| **every day** | I go to the gym **every day**. |
| **once a month** | She visits her parents **once a month**. |
| **twice a year** | We go on vacation **twice a year**. |
| **four times a day** | Take this medicine **four times a day**. |
| **every other week** | We have a meeting **every other week**. |
| **daily** | I read the news **daily**. |
| **monthly** | The magazine is published **monthly**. |

> **Note:** These are also known as **Adverbs of DEFINITE Frequency** because the exact frequency is specified.

## Summary Table

| Type | Position | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Before main verb** | Subject + adverb + verb | I **always remember**. |
| **After To Be** | Subject + to be + adverb | They **are never** pleased. |
| **With auxiliary** | Subject + auxiliary + adverb + verb | She **can sometimes beat** me. |
| **At the start** | Adverb + subject + verb | **Sometimes**, I forget. |
| **Definite frequency** | Any position | I go **every day**. |

## Common Mistakes

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
|--------------|------------|-------------|
| *Always I go to bed early.* | *I **always** go to bed early.* | "Always" cannot start a sentence. |
| *She doesn't hardly ever come.* | *She **hardly ever comes**.* | Use positive verb with "hardly ever." |
| *They don't never say thank you.* | *They **never say** thank you.* | Use positive verb with "never." |
| *I am always go to bed early.* | *I **always go** to bed early.* | Before main verb, not after To Be. |
| *She usually is late.* | *She **is usually** late.* | After To Be. |

## Key Points to Remember

1. Adverbs of frequency describe **how often** we do something.
2. They go **before the main verb** (except To Be).
3. They go **after the verb To Be**.
4. With auxiliary verbs, they go **between the auxiliary and main verb**.
5. **Usually, normally, often, frequently, sometimes, occasionally** can start a sentence.
6. **Always, seldom, rarely, hardly ever, never** cannot start a sentence.
7. Use **hardly ever** and **never** with **positive** verbs.
8. Use **ever** in questions and negative statements.
9. **Definite frequency** expressions specify the exact frequency.
