
# Plural Nouns (Regular and Irregular)

**Plural nouns** are words that refer to **more than one** person, animal, thing, or concept. You can make most nouns plural by adding **-s** or **-es**.

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Singular noun** | Refers to **one** person, place, thing, or idea | one car, a friend, this daisy |
| **Plural noun** | Refers to **two or more** | five cars, a few friends, these daisies |

> **Note:** Only nouns that we can **count** use singular and plural forms. Abstract nouns (knowledge, truth, justice) and uncountable nouns (music, water) do not have plural forms.


## Regular Plurals

### Rules for Forming Regular Plurals

| Ending of the Noun | Plural Ending | Example |
|--------------------|---------------|---------|
| Most nouns | **-s** | book → book**s** |
| -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z | **-es** | bus → bus**es** |
| Consonant + y | **-ies** | frequency → frequenc**ies** |
| -f, -fe | **-ves** | leaf → lea**ves** |
| Consonant + o | **-es** (generally) | potato → potato**es** |
| Vowel + o | **-s** | radio → radio**s** |
| Vowel + z | double z + **-es** | fez → fez**zes** |

**Examples by Rule**

#### 1. Add -s (most nouns)

- thing → things
- year → years
- way → ways
- school → schools

#### 2. Add -es (nouns ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z)

- business → businesses
- rush → rushes
- fox → foxes
- lunch → lunches
- box → boxes
- watch → watches

> **Note:** This makes the plural pronounceable.

#### 3. Change -y to -ies (consonant + y)

- party → parties
- baby → babies
- city → cities
- puppy → puppies
- soliloquy → soliloquies

> **Note:** If the noun ends in **vowel + y**, just add **-s**:
> - play → plays
> - boy → boys
> - day → days

#### 4. Change -f/-fe to -ves

- wife → wives
- wolf → wolves
- knife → knives
- calf → calves
- leaf → leaves

> **Exception:** Some nouns ending in **-f** just add **-s**:
> - roof → roofs
> - chef → chefs
> - chief → chiefs

#### 5. Add -es (consonant + o)

- potato → potatoes
- tomato → tomatoes
- hero → heroes

> **Exception:** Some nouns ending in **consonant + o** just add **-s**:
> - photo → photos
> - avocado → avocados
> - piano → pianos

#### 6. Double z + -es (vowel + z)

- fez → fezzes
- whiz → whizzes

## Irregular Plurals

Irregular nouns do **not** follow the standard **-s** or **-es** rule. They change their form entirely, adopt a unique ending, or keep their singular form.

### 1. Change the vowel

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| foot | feet |
| goose | geese |
| tooth | teeth |
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| mouse | mice |
| louse | lice |

### 2. Add -en

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| child | children |
| ox | oxen |

### 3. Greek nouns ending in -sis → -ses

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| thesis | theses |
| crisis | crises |
| analysis | analyses |
| ellipsis | ellipses |

### 4. Latin nouns ending in -um → -a

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| curriculum | curricula |
| medium | media |
| bacterium | bacteria |
| phenomenon | phenomena / phenomenons |
| schema | schemata / schemas |

### 5. Latin nouns ending in -us → -i

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| alumnus | alumni |
| stimulus | stimuli |
| focus | foci / focuses |
| cactus | cacti / cactuses |

### 6. Same singular and plural form

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| bison | bison |
| buffalo | buffalo / buffaloes |
| deer | deer |
| elk | elk |
| fish | fish |
| moose | moose |
| sheep | sheep |
| series | series |
| species | species |
| scissors | scissors |
| counsel | counsel |
| craft | craft |

> **Note:** Some words like *fish* have contexts where the standard plural is used:
> - *Fishes* is often used to refer to multiple **species** of fish, especially in scientific contexts.

### 7. Other irregular plurals

| Singular | Plural |
|----------|--------|
| index | indexes / indices |
| person | people |
| child | children |

## Plural Nouns vs. Possessive Nouns

| Type | Example | Explanation |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Singular** | boss | one boss |
| **Singular possessive** | boss**'s** | belonging to one boss |
| **Plural** | boss**es** | more than one boss |
| **Plural possessive** | boss**es'** | belonging to multiple bosses |

> **Important:** The apostrophe is the difference between plural and possessive nouns.
> - **Plural nouns** do NOT use an apostrophe (unless they're plural possessive).
> - **Possessive nouns** use an apostrophe.

**Examples:**

| Singular | Singular Possessive | Plural | Plural Possessive |
|----------|---------------------|--------|-------------------|
| boss | boss's | bosses | bosses' |
| treat | treat's | treats | treats' |
| candy | candy's | candies | candies' |

## Plural Nouns vs. Mass Nouns

**Mass nouns** (also called **uncountable** or **noncount** nouns) almost never have plural forms.

| Mass Noun | Countable Version |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Chicken is a good source of protein. | We saw several **chickens** running around. |
| She studied **art** as an undergraduate. | Theater is one of the performing **arts**. |

> **Note:** Some mass nouns have senses in which they are countable, and when used that way, they do have plurals.

## Plurals of Compound Nouns

With **compound nouns** (open, hyphenated, or closed), it is typically the **main part** of the compound that changes form to make the whole compound plural.

| Compound Noun | Plural |
|---------------|--------|
| mother-in-law | mothers-in-law |
| return on investment | returns on investment |
| passerby | passersby |
| toothbrush | toothbrushes |
| wage earner | wage earners |
| apartment building | apartment buildings |

> **Note:** The modifying noun nearly always comes first, and the main noun (usually the second one) changes form.

## Subject-Verb Agreement

Plural nouns acting as subjects need **plural verbs**.

- The Fabelmans often **visit** national parks together. ✅
- The Fabelmans often **visits** national parks together. ❌

> **Tip:** When a prepositional phrase follows the subject, make sure the verb still matches the subject.
> - This category of foods commonly **causes** migraines. ✅
> - This category of foods commonly **cause** migraines. ❌

## Common Mistakes

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
|--------------|------------|-------------|
| *womans* | **women** | Irregular plural |
| *childs* | **children** | Irregular plural |
| *mouses* | **mice** | Irregular plural |
| *sheeps* | **sheep** | Same singular and plural |
| *the teachers books* | **the teachers' books** | Plural possessive needs apostrophe |
| *the teacher's books* (multiple teachers) | **the teachers' books** | Apostrophe placement |
| *less jobs* | **fewer jobs** | Use *fewer* with countable nouns |
| *This category of foods cause...* | **This category of foods causes...** | Subject-verb agreement |

## Key Points

1. Most nouns add **-s** to form the plural.
2. Nouns ending in **-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z** add **-es**.
3. Nouns ending in **consonant + y** change **-y to -ies**.
4. Nouns ending in **-f, -fe** change to **-ves** (with exceptions).
5. Nouns ending in **consonant + o** add **-es** (with exceptions).
6. Some nouns have **irregular** plural forms (child → children, man → men).
7. Some nouns have the **same** singular and plural form (sheep, series, scissors).
8. **Plural nouns** do NOT use an apostrophe; **possessive nouns** do.
9. **Mass nouns** (uncountable) generally do not have plural forms.
10. In **compound nouns**, the main part changes form.
11. Plural nouns need **plural verbs** (subject-verb agreement).
