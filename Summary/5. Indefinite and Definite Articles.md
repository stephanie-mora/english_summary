## Indefinite Articles: "A" and "An"

We use the indefinite article **a/an** with singular nouns when the listener/reader does not know exactly which one we are referring to.
- Police are searching for **a 14-year-old girl**.

We also use it to show that the person or thing is one of a group.
- She is **a pupil at London Road School**.

**Example Text:**
> Police have been looking for **a 14-year-old girl** who has been missing since Friday. 
> 
> Jenny Brown is **a pupil at London Road School**. She is 1.6 meters tall, with short, blonde hair. When she left home, she was wearing **a blue jacket**, **a blue and white blouse**, dark blue jeans and blue shoes. 
> 
> Anyone who has information should contact the local police on 0800 349 781.

### When NOT to use "a/an"

We do **not** use an indefinite article with plural nouns or uncountable nouns:
- She was wearing **blue shoes**. (Plural noun)
- She has short, **blonde hair**. (Uncountable noun)

### Rules

| Article | Use                                    | Examples                           |
|---------|----------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **A**   | Before singular countable nouns beginning with **consonant sounds** | a dog, a teacher, a book, a car, a chair, a bear |
| **An**  | Before singular countable nouns beginning with **vowel sounds** | an umbrella, an ant, an orange, an invitation, an animal, an eagle |

### Exceptions

Some words begin with a vowel letter but have a consonant sound, and vice versa:

| Use "a" (consonant sound) | Use "an" (vowel sound) |
|---------------------------|------------------------|
| a euro                    | an hour                |
| a European                | an honor               |
| a university              |                        |
| a usual                   |                        |
| a unique person           |                        |

### Important Notes

- **An** means *one* or *a single*.
- You **cannot** use a/an with plural nouns.
- Do **not** use a/an with uncountable nouns.
- Use **a** meaning *"one"*, referring to a single object or person, or a single unit of measure.
- Using **"one"** instead of the indefinite article is grammatically correct and adds emphasis to the number.

**Examples**

- I'd like **an orange** and two lemons please.
- I'd like **one orange** and two lemons please.
- The burglar took **a diamond necklace** and some valuable paintings.
- I can think of **a hundred reasons** not to come.
- I need **a kilogram** of sugar.
- I need **one kilogram** of sugar.
- You can't run **a mile** in 5 minutes!
