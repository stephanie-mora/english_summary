| 8      | eight   | 18     | eighteen  |
| 9      | nine    | 19     | nineteen  |

> **Rule:** In English, we use commas (,) to separate thousands: 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000.

## Years

Years are pronounced differently depending on the century.

| Year   | Pronunciation              |
|--------|----------------------------|
| 1980   | nineteen eighty            |
| 1999   | nineteen ninety-nine       |
| 1237   | twelve thirty-seven        |
| 1901   | nineteen o one             |
| 1900   | nineteen hundred           |
| 1800   | eighteen hundred           |
| 2009   | two thousand nine          |
| 2010   | twenty ten                 |
| 2000   | two thousand or twenty hundred |
| 1000   | one thousand or ten hundred |

### Rules for Pronouncing Years

| Period | Rule | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| **1000-1999** | Split into two two-digit numbers | **1980** = nineteen eighty |
| **2000-2009** | Say "two thousand and..." | **2009** = two thousand nine |
| **2010-2099** | Say as a number or split | **2010** = twenty ten / two thousand ten |
| **1900** | Say "nineteen hundred" | |
| **2000** | Say "two thousand" or "twenty hundred" | |

## Ordinal Numbers (Números Ordinales)

Ordinal numbers are used to show order or position (first, second, third...). We use them for dates, ranks, floors, etc.

### 1st - 20th

| Number | Ordinal Word | Number | Ordinal Word |
|--------|--------------|--------|--------------|
| 1st    | first        | 11th   | eleventh     |
| 2nd    | second       | 12th   | twelfth      |
| 3rd    | third        | 13th   | thirteenth   |
| 4th    | fourth       | 14th   | fourteenth   |
| 5th    | fifth        | 15th   | fifteenth    |
| 6th    | sixth        | 16th   | sixteenth    |
| 7th    | seventh      | 17th   | seventeenth  |
| 8th    | eighth       | 18th   | eighteenth   |
| 9th    | ninth        | 19th   | nineteenth   |
| 10th   | tenth        | 20th   | twentieth    |

### More Ordinal Numbers

| Number | Ordinal Word |
|--------|--------------|
| 21st   | twenty-first |
| 30th   | thirtieth    |
| 31st   | thirty-first |
| 40th   | fortieth     |
| 50th   | fiftieth     |
| 100th  | one hundredth |
| 511th  | five hundred and eleventh |

> **Note:** In the written versions, we add **st**, **nd**, **rd** or **th** to the number.
>
> - 1 → **1st** (first)
> - 2 → **2nd** (second)
> - 3 → **3rd** (third)
> - 4-20 → **4th**, **5th**, etc. (fourth, fifth...)

**Examples**

- first → 1st
- second → 2nd
- tenth → 10th
- twenty-first → 21st
- thirty-first - 31st
- one hundredth - 100th 
- five hundred and eleventh - 511th

## Dates (Fechas)

We use numeral adjectives to express dates.

| Date | UK Format | US Format |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| 10th April, 2008 | the tenth of April, two thousand and eight | April (the) tenth, two thousand and eight / April 10, 2008 |
| 1st January, 2020 | the first of January, two thousand and twenty | January first, two thousand twenty |

### Differences: UK vs US

| Format | Example |
|--------|---------|
| **UK** (Day-Month-Year) | 10th April, 2008 |
| **US** (Month-Day-Year) | April 10, 2008 |
| **UK** (Written) | the tenth of April, two thousand and eight |
| **US** (Written) | April (the) tenth, two thousand and eight |


## Centuries and Titles (Siglos y Títulos)

We use ordinal numbers to express centuries and titles of kings, queens, popes, etc.

**Examples**

**Centuries:**

- The 17th century = 1600-1699
- The 21st century = 2000-2099

**Kings and Queens:**

- Charles I = Charles the First
- Elizabeth II = Elizabeth the Second

**Dialog Example:**

> **Person A:** Did you know that **Charles the First** lived in the **seventeenth century**?
> **Person B:** Really? You're so clever! The **21st century** is a lot more interesting with you in it.

#### Key Points to Remember

1. Use **hyphens** for compound numbers: twenty-one, thirty-five.   
2. Use **commas** for thousands: 1,000, 10,000, 100,000.
3. **Years** can be said in two ways depending on the century.
4. **Ordinal numbers** show order/position.
5. Use **ordinal numbers** for dates (UK and US formats differ).
