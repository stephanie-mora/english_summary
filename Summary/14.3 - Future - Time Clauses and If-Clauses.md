
# Future: Time Clauses and If-Clauses

When we talk about the future in **time clauses** and **if-clauses**, we use the **Simple Present** — NOT **will** or **be going to**.

## Time Clauses

**Common time words:** before, after, when, as soon as, until, while

> **Rule:** Use the **Simple Present** in a future time clause.

**Examples**

- **Before I go** to class tomorrow, I**'m going to** eat breakfast.
- I**'m going to** eat breakfast **before I go** to class tomorrow.
- **Before I go** home tonight, I**'m going to** stop at the market.
- I**'m going to** eat dinner at 6:00 tonight. **After I eat** dinner, I**'m going to** study in my room.
- I**'ll** give Rita your message **when I see** her.
- It's raining right now. **As soon as the rain stops**, I**'m going to** walk downtown.
- I**'ll** stay home **until the rain stops**.
- **While you're** at school tomorrow, I**'ll** be at work.

## If-Clauses

> **Rule:** When the meaning is future, use the **Simple Present** in an if-clause.

### Examples

- Maybe it will rain tomorrow. **If it rains** tomorrow, I**'m going to** stay home.

## Summary

| Clause Type | Verb Form |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Main clause** | will / be going to |
| **Time clause** | Simple Present |
| **If-clause (future meaning)** | Simple Present |

## Key Points

1. Use the **Simple Present** in future time clauses.
2. Use the **Simple Present** in if-clauses with future meaning.
3. **Never** use **will** or **be going to** in a time clause or if-clause.
4. Time words: before, after, when, as soon as, until, while.
5. If-clause formula: **if + subject + verb (Simple Present)**
