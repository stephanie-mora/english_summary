
# Common Uncountable Nouns

Some nouns in English are **uncountable**. They refer to things which cannot be counted separately. We cannot use the indefinite article (a/an) with them.

To make an uncountable noun countable, we often use the construction:

> **a/an + noun container/measure + of + uncountable noun**

**Example:** A glass of water

### Rules for Making Uncountable Nouns Countable

To make an uncountable noun countable, we use the structure:

> **a/an + container/measurement + of + uncountable noun**

## Common Containers and Measurements

| Container/Measurement | Used With |
|-----------------------|-----------|
| a piece of            | advice, furniture, information, homework, music, news, paper, research, work, gossip |
| a bottle of           | water, wine, oil, alcohol, petrol, vinegar, washing up liquid |
| a glass/cup of        | water, milk, tea, coffee, juice |
| a slice/piece of      | cheese, meat, beef, pork, bread |
| a jar of              | honey, jam, peanut butter |
| a bar of              | soap, chocolate, gold, silver |
| a pinch of            | salt, pepper, sugar |
| a grain of            | rice, sand, salt |
| a drop of             | water, blood, rain, oil |
| a flash of            | lightning |
| a clap of             | thunder |
| a gust of             | wind, air |
| a blade of            | grass |
| a sheet of            | paper |
| a pair of             | trousers, glasses, jeans |
| a tube of             | toothpaste |
| a ball of             | wool, string |
| a loaf of             | bread |

**Examples**

- **a piece of** advice → *Can you give me a piece of advice?*
- **a bottle of** water → *I bought a bottle of water.*
- **a slice of** cheese → *She ate a slice of cheese.*
- **a jar of** honey → *We need a jar of honey.*
- **a bar of** soap → *He bought a bar of soap.*
- **a pinch of** salt → *Add a pinch of salt.*
- **a grain of** rice → *There is a grain of rice on the floor.*
- **a drop of** blood → *There was a drop of blood on the shirt.*
- **a flash of** lightning → *We saw a flash of lightning.*
- **a clap of** thunder → *A loud clap of thunder woke me up.*
- **a gust of** wind → *A gust of wind blew the leaves away.*
- **a blade of** grass → *She picked a blade of grass.*
- **a sheet of** paper → *He wrote on a sheet of paper.*
- **a pair of** trousers → *I need a new pair of trousers.*
- **a tube of** toothpaste → *She bought a tube of toothpaste.*
- **a ball of** wool → *She knitted with a ball of wool.*

## Important Notes

### 1. Uncountable nouns always take singular verbs

- *The news **is** good.*
- *The information **was** useful.*
- *The furniture **is** new.*

### 2. Quantity expressions

| Expression | Use |
|------------|-----|
| **much** | with uncountable nouns |
| **a little** | with uncountable nouns |
| **a lot of** | with both countable and uncountable |
| **some** | with both (affirmative) |
| **any** | with both (negative/questions) |
| **no** | with both |

### 3. Nouns that can be countable or uncountable (different meaning)

| Uncountable (meaning) | Countable (meaning) |
|-----------------------|---------------------|
| **hair** (hair in general) | **a hair** (a single strand) |
| **experience** (experience in general) | **an experience** (an event) |
| **work** (work in general) | **a work** (a work of art) |
| **coffee/tea** (the drink) | **a coffee/tea** (a cup of) |
| **glass** (material) | **a glass** (a drinking vessel) |
| **paper** (material) | **a paper** (a document/newspaper) |
| **time** (time in general) | **a time** (an occasion) |
| **light** (light) | **a light** (a lamp) |
| **room** (space) | **a room** (a bedroom) |
| **chicken** (meat) | **a chicken** (a live animal) |
