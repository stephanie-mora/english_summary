
# Present Progressive (Present Continuous)

The **Present Progressive** (also called **Present Continuous**) puts emphasis on the **course or duration** of an action. It is used for:

- Actions happening **at the moment of speaking**
- Actions taking place **only for a short period of time**
- **Definite plans** for the future

### Affirmative Forms

> **Structure:** **Subject + am/is/are + verb + -ing + complement**

| Subject | Auxiliary | Verb + -ing | Example |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| I | am ('m) | eating | I**'m eating**. |
| You | are ('re) | eating | You**'re eating**. |
| He | is ('s) | eating | He**'s eating**. |
| She | is ('s) | eating | She**'s eating**. |
| It | is ('s) | eating | It**'s eating**. |
| We | are ('re) | eating | We**'re eating**. |
| You | are ('re) | eating | You**'re eating**. |
| They | are ('re) | eating | They**'re eating**. |

### Negative Forms

> **Structure:** **Subject + am/is/are + not + verb + -ing + complement**

| Subject | Auxiliary | Contraction | Example |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| I | am not | I'm not | I**'m not eating**. |
| You | are not | aren't | You**aren't eating**. |
| He | is not | isn't | He**isn't eating**. |
| She | is not | isn't | She**isn't eating**. |
| It | is not | isn't | It**isn't eating**. |
| We | are not | aren't | We**aren't eating**. |
| You | are not | aren't | You**aren't eating**. |
| They | are not | aren't | They**aren't eating**. |

### Interrogative Forms

> **Structure:** **Am/Is/Are + subject + verb + -ing + complement?**

| Auxiliary | Subject | Verb + -ing | Example |
|-----------|---------|-------------|---------|
| Am | I | eating? | **Am** I **eating**? |
| Are | you | eating? | **Are** you **eating**? |
| Is | he | eating? | **Is** he **eating**? |
| Is | she | eating? | **Is** she **eating**? |
| Is | it | eating? | **Is** it **eating**? |
| Are | we | eating? | **Are** we **eating**? |
| Are | you | eating? | **Are** you **eating**? |
| Are | they | eating? | **Are** they **eating**? |

### Short Answers

| Affirmative | Negative |
|-------------|----------|
| Yes, I **am**. | No, I**'m not**. |
| Yes, you **are**. | No, you **aren't**. |
| Yes, he **is**. | No, he **isn't**. |
| Yes, she **is**. | No, she **isn't**. |
| Yes, it **is**. | No, it **isn't**. |
| Yes, we **are**. | No, we **aren't**. |
| Yes, they **are**. | No, they **aren't**. |

## Spelling Rules for -ing

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Add **-ing** to most verbs | play → play**ing**, cry → cry**ing**, bark → bark**ing** |
| Verbs ending in **-e**: remove the **-e** and add **-ing** | slide → slid**ing**, ride → rid**ing** |
| Verbs ending in **-ie**: change **-ie** to **y** and add **-ing** | die → d**ying**, tie → t**ying** |
| Verbs ending in **CVC** (stressed): double the last letter + **-ing** | beg → be**gging**, begin → begi**nning** |
| Verbs ending in **CVC** (not stressed): just add **-ing** | enter → enter**ing** |

## Uses of the Present Progressive

### 1. Actions Happening at the Moment of Speaking

- Where is Mary? She **is having** a bath. (Not: *she has a bath*)
- What **are** you **doing** at the moment? You **are reading** this lesson.
- We **are discussing** the project at the moment.

### 2. Actions That Have Started but Not Finished

- I **am reading** a book; it's a nice book. (I started reading it but haven't finished it yet.)

### 3. Definite Plans for the Future

- Jim**'s leaving** for Brussels this evening.
- **Are** you **participating** in the competition next week?
- I**'m meeting** Peter for a drink later.

### 4. Repeated Actions (with "always" — often shows annoyance)

- Her husband **is always complaining** about his health.
- That company **is always selling** some cheap gadget. (negative meaning)

## Verbs NOT Used in the Present Progressive

Some verbs are **normally not used** in the present progressive. These are **stative verbs** (verbs of state).

**Examples:** be, believe, belong, hate, hear, like, love, mean, prefer, remain, realize, see, seem, smell, think, understand, want, wish

❌ *He is wanting to buy a new car.*
✅ *He **wants** to buy a new car.*

❌ *I am knowing the answer.*
✅ *I **know** the answer.*

> **Note:** Some stative verbs can be used in continuous form with a **different meaning**:
> - *I **have** a car.* (possession — stative)
> - *I**'m having** lunch.* (experience — action)
> - *I **think** it's good.* (opinion — stative)
> - *I**'m thinking** about it.* (process — action)

## Structure Summary

| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Affirmative** | Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing | She **is eating**. |
| **Negative** | Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing | She **isn't eating**. |
| **Yes/No Question** | Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing? | **Is** she **eating**? |
| **Wh- Question** | Wh- + am/is/are + subject + verb-ing? | **What** is she **eating**? |
| **Short Answer** | Yes/No + subject + am/is/are | Yes, she **is**. |

## Wh- Questions

> **Structure:** **Wh- word + am/is/are + subject + verb + -ing + complement?**

| Wh Word | Auxiliary | Subject | Verb + -ing | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|-------------|---------|
| Who | am | I | meeting | **Who** am I **meeting** with today? |
| What | is | he/she/it | doing | **What** is he **doing** right now? |
| When | are | you/we/they | choosing | **When** are you **choosing** the colors? |
| Which | are | you | taking | **Which** route are you **taking**? |
| Why | is | she | bleeding | **Why** is she **bleeding**? |

## Tag Questions

Tag questions are short questions added to the end of a sentence.

> **Structure:** Positive sentence + isn't/aren't + pronoun?

| Sentence | Tag Question |
|----------|--------------|
| Terry is driving to the post office, | **isn't she**? |
| All the kids are going on the field trip, | **aren't they**? |
| I'm buying tickets for everyone, | **aren't I**? |

> **Note:** For sentences in the first person (I), use the tag **aren't I?**

**Negative sentences + is/are + pronoun?**

| Sentence | Tag Question |
|----------|--------------|
| Latika's not working at IBM anymore, | **is she**? |
| Tomer and Guy aren't going on the trek, | **are they**? |

## Common Mistakes

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
|--------------|------------|-------------|
| *He is wanting a new car.* | *He **wants** a new car.* | "Want" is stative. |
| *I am knowing the answer.* | *I **know** the answer.* | "Know" is stative. |
| *She is having a car.* | *She **has** a car.* | "Have" for possession is stative. |
| *We are producing high-end plastic pipes.* | *We **produce** high-end plastic pipes.* | General fact, not action in progress. |
| *Are you understanding?* | *Do you **understand**?* | "Understand" is stative. |
| *I am loving this party!* | *I **love** this party!* | "Love" is stative. |
| *She is seeming nice.* | *She **seems** nice.* | "Seem" is stative. |

## Key Points to Remember

1. Use **am/is/are + verb-ing** for actions happening now.
2. Use **contractions** ('m, 're, 's) in spoken English.
3. Use **stative verbs** in Simple Present, NOT Present Progressive.
4. Use Present Progressive for **definite future plans**.
5. Use **always** with Present Progressive to show annoyance.
6. **Tag questions**: positive sentence + isn't/aren't + pronoun.
7. For first person (I), use **aren't I?** in tag questions.
8. Some stative verbs can be used in continuous form with a **different meaning**.
