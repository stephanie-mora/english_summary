
# Future - Be Going To vs. Will

Both **be going to** and **will** are used to talk about the future, but they have different uses. The choice depends on **how the speaker sees the situation**.

## Uses

### 1. Predictions (both can be used)

When making **predictions** about the future, **be going to** and **will** mean the same, but **be going to** suggests there is **evidence** for the prediction.

| Form | Example | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Will** | She **will** succeed because she works hard. | Prediction based on belief |
| **Be Going To** | She **is going to** succeed because she works hard. | Prediction based on evidence |
| **Will** | I think Brazil **will** win the World Cup. | Belief |
| **Be Going To** | Look at those clouds! It**'s going to rain**. | Evidence (clouds) |

### 2. Prior Plans → Be Going To

Use **be going to** (NOT will) to express a **prior plan** (a plan made before the moment of speaking).

> - I bought some wood because I **am going to** build a bookcase for my apartment.
  *(The speaker planned to build a bookcase before speaking.)*

> - They **are going to** move to Manchester.
> - I**'m going to** drive to work today.

### 3. Decisions at the Moment → Will

Use **will** (NOT be going to) to express a **decision made at the moment of speaking**.
> - This chair is too heavy for you to carry alone. I**'ll** help you.
  *(The speaker decided to help at the immediate present moment.)*

> - "Oh, we don't have sugar." "Don't worry, I**'ll** buy some."

### 4. Promises, Offers, Requests, Threats → Will

**Will** is used for:

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| **Promises** | I **will** help you whenever you need me. |
| **Offers** | I**'ll** carry that bag for you. |
| **Requests** | **Will** you open the door, please? |
| **Threats** | If you do that again, I**'ll** tell Mum. |
| **Refusals** | I **won't** lend him my car. |

### 5. Future Facts → Will

- The president **will** visit the Vatican next November.

### 6. Be Going To for Predictions Based on Evidence

- Be careful! You **are going to** fall. (= I can see that you might fall.)
- Look at those black clouds. I think it**'s going to** rain. (= I can see that it will rain.)

## Examples

| Situation | Correct Form | Why |
|-----------|--------------|-----|
| Prediction | It **is going to** rain. / It **will** rain. | Both possible |
| Evidence-based prediction | Look at those clouds! It**'s going to** rain. | Evidence (clouds) |
| Prior plan | I**'m going to** visit my parents next week. | Plan made before speaking |
| Decision now | I**'ll** have the chicken, please. | Decision at the moment |
| Promise | I **won't** tell anyone. | Promise |
| Offer | I**'ll** carry your bags. | Offer |
| Request | **Will** you help me? | Request |

## Key Points

1. **Be going to** and **will** are both used for predictions.
2. **Be going to** suggests there is **evidence** for the prediction.
3. Use **be going to** for plans made before speaking.
4. Use **will** for decisions made at the moment of speaking.
5. Use **will** for promises, offers, requests, threats, and refusals.
6. Use **be going to** for predictions based on what you can see.
7. Remember the structure: **be going to + base verb** / **will + base verb**.

### Exercise 1: Choose the correct form (be going to or will)

2. I `______________` (help) you with your homework. (decision now)
3. She `______________` (study) medicine next year. (prior plan)
