## Structures

### 1. Positive Sentences

> **Structure:** **Subject + verb to be + complement**

- I am a student.
- He is my friend.
- She is at home.
- We are brothers.
- You are a good student.
- Tatiana is a good driver.
- They are classmates.

### 2. Negative Sentences

To make a sentence negative, we add **not** after the verb **to be**. We can use contractions to sound more natural.

> **Structure:** **Subject + verb to be + not + complement**

- You aren't a doctor.
- He is not my brother.
- We are not good friends.
- She isn't my professor.
- They're not here.

**Negative Contractions:**

| Full Form | Contraction 1 | Contraction 2 |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| I am not  | I'm not       | —             |
| You are not | You aren't  | You're not    |
| He is not | He isn't      | He's not      |
| She is not | She isn't    | She's not     |
| It is not | It isn't      | It's not      |
| We are not | We aren't    | We're not     |
| You are not | You aren't  | You're not    |
| They are not | They aren't | They're not   |

> **Note:** Both contractions are correct and commonly used. Example: *He isn't here.* = *He's not here.*

### 3. Yes/No Questions
To form a yes/no question, we **invert** the subject and the verb **to be**. The verb comes first, followed by the subject.

> **Structure:** **Verb to be + subject + complement?**

| Question                | Answer          |
|-------------------------|-----------------|
| Are you my friend?      | Yes, I am       |
| Is he Manuel?           | No, he isn't    |
| Are they janitors?      | Yes, they are   |
| Is Lucia at work?       | No, she isn't   |
| Are you outside?        | No, we aren't   |

**Short Answers:**

- **Affirmative:** Yes, + subject + am/is/are → *Yes, I am. / Yes, she is.*
- **Negative:** No, + subject + am/is/are + not → *No, he isn't. / No, they aren't.*

> **Important:** 
> In short affirmative answers, we **do not use contractions** 
> - *Yes, I am.* ✅ 
> - *Yes, I'm.* ❌). 
> 
> In short negative answers, we **can use contractions** 
> - *No, I'm not.* ✅).


### 4. Wh- Questions Structure

Wh- questions ask for specific information. They begin with a question word (What, Where, Who, etc.), followed by the verb **to be**, then the subject.

> **Structure:** **Wh- word + verb to be + subject + complement?**

| Question                | Answer                           |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Where are you?          | I am in the park.                |
| Where is your friend?   | He / She is in the classroom.    |
| What is it?             | It is a book.                    |
| Who are they?           | They are my parents.             |

#### Key Points to Remember

1. **What** → things / ideas
2. **Where** → places
3. **Who** → people
4. **When** → time
5. **Why** → reasons
6. **How** → manner / condition
7. **Whom** → object of a verb/preposition (formal)
8. **Whose** → possession
9. **Which** → choices / selection
10. **How many** → countable nouns
11. **How much** → uncountable nouns
12. **How often** → frequency
13. **How long** → duration
14. **How far** → distance
15. **How come** → informal "why"

### 5. Uses of Verb "To Be"

The verb **to be** is used in many different situations. Here are the most common uses:

| Use | Explanation | Example |
|-----|-------------|---------|
| **Profession** | To say what someone does for a living | *She **is** a doctor.* |
| **Nationality** | To say where someone is from | *They **are** Mexican.* |
| **Age** | To say how old someone is | *I **am** 25 years old.* |
| **Location** | To say where someone or something is | *We **are** at home.* |
| **Characteristics** | To describe physical appearance or personality | *He **is** tall and handsome.* |
| **Marital status** | To say if someone is married, single, etc. | *She **is** married.* |
| **Time** | To tell the time | *It **is** 3 o'clock.* |
| **Weather** | To describe the weather | *It **is** cold today.* |
| **Feelings** | To describe emotions | *I **am** happy.* |
| **Price** | To talk about cost | *How much **is** it?* |

### 6. There is / There are (Hay / Existe)

We use **there is** and **there are** to say that something exists or to talk about the presence of something.

**Structure:**

- **There is** + singular noun / uncountable noun
- **There are** + plural noun 

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Affirmative** | *There **is** a book on the table.* |
| **Affirmative** | *There **are** two books on the table.* |
| **Negative** | *There **isn't** any milk.* |
| **Negative** | *There **aren't** any chairs.* |
| **Question** | *Is there a bank near here?* |
| **Question** | *Are there any restaurants?* |

**Questions:**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Is there a cafe? | Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. |
| Are there any cafes? | Yes, there are. / No, there aren't. |

## Key Points to Remember

1. **I** → **am** (*I am a student.*)
2. **He/She/It** → **is** (*She is my friend.*)
3. **We/You/They** → **are** (*We are brothers.*)
4. To make **negative**: add **not** after the verb.
5. To make **questions**: invert the subject and verb.
6. **There is** → singular / **There are** → plural.
