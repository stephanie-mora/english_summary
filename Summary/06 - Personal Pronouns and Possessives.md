# Personal Pronouns and Possessives

## What is a Noun?

A **noun** is a word that represents a **person, place, thing, or idea**. We can think of nouns as "naming words" — everything we can see or talk about is represented by a word, and that word is a noun.

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **People** | soldier, Alan, cousin, lawyer |
| **Places** | house, London, factory, shelter |
| **Things (Objects)** | table, London Bridge, chisel, nitrogen, month, inch |
| **Things (Animals)** | aardvark, rat, shark, Mickey |
| **Things (Ideas)** | confusion, kindness, faith, joy |

### What is a Pronoun?

A **pronoun** is a word that **replaces a noun** (or a noun phrase) to avoid repetition when talking about something or someone again.

> *The house is red.* → ***It** is red.*
> *Mary is upset today.* → ***She** is upset today.*
> *Susan and Pete want to go.* → ***They** want to go.*

### Why Do We Use Pronouns?

We use pronouns to **avoid repeating nouns** when it is clear what we are referring to.

> *Mum's calling. **She's** in Turkey.*
> *How's Daisy? Give **her** my love.*

## Types of Pronouns

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| **Personal** | I, you, he, she, it, we, they |
| **Object** | me, you, him, her, it, us, them |
| **Possessive Pronouns** | mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs |
| **Possessive Adjectives** | my, your, his, her, its, our, their |
| **Reflexive** | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves |
| **Demonstrative** | this, these, that, those |
| **Interrogative** | who, whom, whose, what, which |
| **Relative** | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| **Indefinite** | anybody, someone, nothing, all, both, few, many, etc. |
| **Reciprocal** | each other, one another |

## Subject Pronouns

**Subject pronouns** replace the **subject** of the verb — the person or thing doing the action.

| Person | Subject Pronoun | Meaning |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| 1st Singular | I | Yo |
| 2nd Singular | You | Tú / Usted |
| 3rd Singular (Male) | He | Él |
| 3rd Singular (Female) | She | Ella |
| 3rd Singular (Thing) | It | Eso / Ello |
| 1st Plural | We | Nosotros / Nosotras |
| 2nd Plural | You | Ustedes / Vosotros |
| 3rd Plural | They | Ellos / Ellas |

**Examples:**

- **Mary** is terribly upset today. → **She** is terribly upset today.
- **Tom's house** is red and white. → **It** is red and white.
- **Susan and Pete** want to go to the party. → **They** want to go to the party.
- Is **Dad** travelling to London next week? → Is **he** travelling to London next week?

### Tips for Subject Pronouns

1. **Subject pronouns go before verbs** (except in questions).
2. **Subject pronouns are required** in English — they cannot be omitted like in Spanish.
3. **It** is used as a "dummy" pronoun for weather, time, and general statements.
4. **Gender matters** for 3rd person singular: **he** (man), **she** (woman), **it** (thing/animal), **they** (unknown/neutral).

## Object Pronouns

**Object pronouns** replace a noun that is the **object** of a verb or a noun that comes after a **preposition**.

| Person | Object Pronoun | Meaning |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| 1st Singular | me | a mí (me) |
| 2nd Singular | you | a ti (te) |
| 3rd Singular (Male) | him | a él (lo/le) |
| 3rd Singular (Female) | her | a ella (la/le) |
| 3rd Singular (Thing) | it | a ello (lo/se) |
| 1st Plural | us | a nosotros (nos) |
| 2nd Plural | you | a vosotros (os) |
| 3rd Plural | them | a ellos (los/les) |

**Examples:**

- I gave **my husband** a very nice surprise. → I gave **him** a very nice surprise.
- All the students did **their exercises** yesterday. → All the students did **them** yesterday.
- I will go to the party with **Sam**. → I will go to the party with **him**.
- I like my eggs well done. Please cook **them** longer than usual.
- My dad Eddy is picking **me** up today, so I'll see **him** after school.

> **Note:** Object pronouns always go **after** the verb.

## Possessive Adjectives

**Possessive adjectives** accompany a noun in a noun phrase to show ownership.

| Person | Possessive Adjective | Meaning |
|--------|---------------------|---------|
| 1st Singular | my | mi |
| 2nd Singular | your | tu |
| 3rd Singular (Male) | his | su |
| 3rd Singular (Female) | her | su |
| 3rd Singular (Thing) | its | su |
| 1st Plural | our | nuestro/a |
| 2nd Plural | your | vuestro/a |
| 3rd Plural | their | su |

**Examples:**

- It is **Tom's** house. → It is **his** house.
- This is **Susan's** car. → This is **her** car.
- Those are **my parents'** keys. → Those are **their** keys.
- **My** friend and I write fanfic together. **Our** fanfic is hilarious.
- I can never read **her** handwriting!

> **Note:** Possessive adjectives go **before** the noun they describe.
 
## Possessive Pronouns

**Possessive pronouns** replace a noun (or a noun phrase) and show ownership. They are **not** followed by a noun.

| Person | Possessive Pronoun | Meaning |
|--------|-------------------|---------|
| 1st Singular | mine | mío/a/os/as |
| 2nd Singular | yours | tuyo/a/os/as |
| 3rd Singular (Male) | his | suyo/a/os/as |
| 3rd Singular (Female) | hers | suyo/a/os/as |
| 3rd Singular (Thing) | — | — |
| 1st Plural | ours | nuestro/a/os/as |
| 2nd Plural | yours | vuestro/a/os/as |
| 3rd Plural | theirs | suyo/a/os/as |

**Examples:**
- This house is **Tom's**. → This house is **his**.
- Whose car is this? It's **mine**.
- This is her idea. → This idea is **hers**.
- I just bought 2 delicious cupcakes. You can have one, but don't touch **mine**!
- The referees locked their bikes near Dawn's bike. **Hers** is red, and **theirs** are both black.

> **Important:** There are **no apostrophes** in possessive pronouns: *yours, hers, ours, theirs* (NOT *your's, her's, our's, their's*).

## Possessive Pronouns vs. Possessive Adjectives

| Possessive Adjectives | Possessive Pronouns |
|-----------------------|---------------------|
| **Followed by a noun** | **NOT followed by a noun** |
| This is **my** book. | This book is **mine**. |
| That is **your** car. | That car is **yours**. |
| **His** dog is friendly. | The friendly dog is **his**. |
| **Her** pen is blue. | The blue pen is **hers**. |
| **Our** house is big. | The big house is **ours**. |
| **Their** children are young. | The young children are **theirs**. |

## Possessives with Apostrophe ('s)

A possessive form is spelled with **'s** at the end.

**Examples:**

- Lisa**'s** essay
- England**'s** navy
- my brother**'s** girlfriend
- children**'s** shoes
- women**'s** clothing
- the aircraft**'s** black box
- somebody**'s** umbrella
- a week**'s** work
- my money**'s** worth

This rule applies in **most cases**, even with a name ending in **s**:

- Thomas**'s** job
- the bus**'s** arrival
- James**'s** fiancée
- Steve Davis**'s** victory

### Exception 1: Plural Nouns Ending in -s

A **plural noun** which already ends in **s** takes only a following apostrophe (**'**).

**Examples:**
- the girls**'** excitement
- my parents**'** wedding
- both players**'** injuries
- the Klingons**'** attack
- the ladies**'** room
- two weeks**'** work

> **Reason:** We don't pronounce these words with two esses, so we don't write two esses: nobody says *the girls's excitement*.

> **Note:** Plurals that **don't** end in **s** take the ordinary form:
> - **children's** shoes
> - **women's** clothing

### Exception 2: Names Ending in -s

A **name ending in s** takes only an apostrophe if the possessive form is **not pronounced** with an extra **s**.

**Examples:**
- Socrates**'** philosophy
- Saint Saens**'** music
- Ulysses**'** companions
- Aristophanes**'** plays

> **Reason:** We don't say *Ulysses's companions*, so we don't write the extra **s**.

### Exception 3: Pronouns

**Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe.**

| ✅ Correct | ❌ Incorrect |
|------------|--------------|
| He lost **his** book. | — |
| Which seats are **ours**? | — |
| The bull lowered **its** head. | *The bull lowered **it's** head.* |
| **Whose** are these spectacles? | ***Who's** are these spectacles?* |
| One must choose **one's** words carefully. | — |

> **Important:** 
> - **Its** = possessive (never takes an apostrophe)
> - **It's** = contraction of *it is* or *it has*
> - There is **no** English word spelled ***its'***
> - **Whose** = possessive (never takes an apostrophe)
> - **Who's** = contraction of *who is* or *who has*

**Examples:**
- The bull lowered **its** head. ✅
- *The bull lowered **it's** head.* ❌
- **Whose** are these spectacles? ✅
- ***Who's** are these spectacles?* ❌
- **Who's** your friend? ✅ (contraction of *who is*)
- **It's** raining. ✅ (contraction of *it is*)

### Important Note

When you add an apostrophe-s or an apostrophe alone to form a possessive, the thing that comes **before** the apostrophe must be a **real English word** and it must also be the **right English word**.

| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
|--------------|------------|-------------|
| *ladie's shoes* | **ladies'** shoes | There is no such word as *ladie*. |
| *lady's shoes* | **ladies'** shoes | The shop sells shoes for **ladies**, not for *lady*. |

> **Compare:** *that **lady's** shoes* — ✅ correct (one lady)

### Special Case: Menswear

| Form | Example |
|------|---------|
| With apostrophe | **men's** clothing |
| Without apostrophe | **menswear** (single word) |

> **Note:** *Menswear* is regarded as a single word in English. But we do **not** write *womenswear* or *childrenswear* — just this one exception.

## The Apostrophe S ('s) — Possessive Case / Genitive Case

### What is the Genitive Case?

The **genitive case** (also called the **possessive case**) is when we add **apostrophe S ('s)** to show **possession** — that something belongs to another, or a type of relationship between things.

> **Woodward's house** = The house of Woodward
> **Your brother's friend** = The friend of your brother

### The Meaning of X's Y

> **X's Y** =
> - The Y of X
> - The Y belonging to X (Y is normally a thing)
> - The Y which has some relation with X (Y is often a person)

### When to Use 's

We normally use **'s** with:
- **People**: *my mother's house*
- **Animals**: *the dog's tail*
- **Places**: *London's museums*
- **Organizations and companies** (which suggest a group of people): *the company's policy*

> **Note:** It is **not common** to use **'s** with non-living things.

### Rules for Forming the Possessive

#### 1. Singular Nouns

Add **'s** (apostrophe + S).

- My mother**'s** house is next to the beach. (= the house of my mother)
- Jason**'s** car was stolen last night. (= the car of Jason)
- Tomorrow, we're all going to see the museum**'s** new art exhibit.

#### 2. Plural Nouns Ending in -s

Only add the **apostrophe '** (without the S).

- The two sisters**'** house is next to mine. (= the house of the two sisters)
- The plumbers**'** tools were rusty. (= the tools of the plumbers)
- The players**'** boots were dirty and smelly after the game. (= the boots of the players)

> **Note:** The pronunciation is the same for certain possessives:
> - *My friend**'s** house* = the house of my friend = **1 friend**
> - *My friends**'** house* = the house of my friends = **2 or more friends**
>
> You can usually distinguish whether the speaker is referring to one or two friends by listening to the context.

#### 3. Plural Nouns NOT Ending in -s

Add **'s**.

- Be careful not to trip over the children**'s** toys. (= the toys of the children)
- The women**'s** bathroom is currently flooded with water.
- The presidential candidate is often called the people**'s** favorite politician.

#### 4. Singular Noun Ending in -s

It depends…

**a. Most names: add 's (apostrophe S)**

- They had a really good time at James**'s** barbecue last Friday.
- We spent the day admiring Frances**'s** new car.

**b. Classical or religious names: add ' (only the apostrophe)**

- Jesus**'** disciples carried out the teachings of Jesus.
- Sophocles**'** plays are still performed today.

#### 5. Possessive Nouns as Part of a Phrase

Sometimes more than one word/noun is a possessive. The same rules as above are still valid:

- The King of Sparta**'s** wife was called Helen.
- The President of Chile**'s** speech was very long.
- I accidentally took someone else**'s** bag home by mistake.
- I had to give my boss three weeks**'** notice that I was leaving the company.

**Two owners of something:** add **'s** to the **final name**:
- Rick and Steve**'s** car is quite old.

**Each person owns something:** add **'s** to **both names**:
- Rick**'s** and Steve**'s** cars are quite old. (Notice the verb is in plural form.)

#### 6. No Noun

If the meaning is clear, we can use the possessive **without a noun** after it.

- Her hair is longer than Jill**'s**. (= Jill's hair)
- We ate at Billy**'s** last night. (= Billy's Diner or Billy's house)
- Whose bag is this? It**'s** Jane**'s**. (= Jane's bag)

## Reflexive Pronouns

**Reflexive pronouns** refer back to the person or thing doing the action when the same person or thing is both subject and object.

| 1st Singular | myself | me, yo mismo |
| 2nd Singular | yourself | te, tú mismo |
| 3rd Singular (Male) | himself | se, a él mismo |
| 3rd Singular (Female) | herself | se, a ella misma |
| 3rd Singular (Thing) | itself | se, a ello mismo |
| 1st Plural | ourselves | nos, nosotros mismos |
| 2nd Plural | yourselves | os, vosotros mismos |
| 3rd Plural | themselves | se, a ellos mismos |

**Examples:**

- The baby likes to see **himself** in the mirror.
- I was so proud of **myself** after throwing the surprise party!
- We can't help but look at **ourselves** during the whole Zoom meeting.

## Uses of Personal Pronouns

Personal pronouns are used as:

1. **Subjects** → *We saw an accident.*
2. **Objects** → *Anna saw it too.*
3. **To show possession** → *I have my pen. Sue has hers. / Her pen is blue.*

## Singular vs. Plural Reference

- Use a **singular pronoun** to refer to a **singular noun**.
  - *I have a book. **It** is on my desk.* (book → it, both singular)
- Use a **plural pronoun** to refer to a **plural noun**.
  - *I have some books. **They** are on my desk.* (books → they, both plural)

## Special Uses of "They"

Sometimes we use **they** instead of **he** or **she**, **them** instead of **him** or **her**, and **their** instead of **his** or **hers**:

1. **When gender is unknown or irrelevant:**
   - When you meet your new teacher, **they** will give you the books.
   - If anyone asks where I am, tell **them** I'm in Istanbul this week.
   - Someone left **their** gloves in the classroom.
2. **For groups of people:**
   - The English cricket team lost again. **They** were rubbish.
3. **For nonbinary people:**
   - I'm sure Ash will be there and **they'll** be able to help us.

## Special Uses of "It"

- **It** is used for things and animals that aren't pets.
- **It** is also a "dummy" pronoun for general statements:
  - **It** is nice to meet you.
  - **It** is not easy to learn grammar rules.
- **It** is used for weather and time:
  - **It** is sunny.
  - **It** is going to rain tomorrow.
  - **It** is 3 o'clock.

## Common Mistakes
| ❌ Incorrect | ✅ Correct | Explanation |
|--------------|------------|-------------|
| *That's not **your's**.* | *That's not **yours**.* | No apostrophe in possessive pronouns. |
| *The dog hurt **it's** paw.* | *The dog hurt **its** paw.* | **Its** = possessive; **it's** = it is. |
| *Me and John went to the store.* | *John and **I** went to the store.* | Use subject pronoun as subject. |
| *Between you and **I**...* | *Between you and **me**...* | Use object pronoun after preposition. |
| *Her's is the red one.* | ***Hers** is the red one.* | No apostrophe in possessive pronouns. |
| *They're going to **they're** house.* | *They're going to **their** house.* | **Their** = possessive; **they're** = they are. |
| *The team lost **its** game.* | *The team lost **its** game.* | ✅ Correct (collective noun = singular) |
| *Give the book to **she**.* | *Give the book to **her**.* | Use object pronoun after preposition. |
| *The dog hurt **it's** paw.* | *The dog hurt **its** paw.* | **Its** = possessive; **it's** = it is. |
| ***Who's** are these?* | ***Whose** are these?* | **Whose** = possessive; **who's** = who is. |
| *the **girls's** excitement* | *the **girls'** excitement* | Plural ending in -s takes only apostrophe. |
| ***ladie's** shoes* | ***ladies'** shoes* | Must be a real English word before apostrophe. |
| *That is **her's**.* | *That is **hers**.* | No apostrophe in possessive pronouns. |

## Key Points to Remember

1. **Pronouns** replace nouns to avoid repetition.
2. **Subject pronouns** replace the subject (before the verb).
3. **Object pronouns** replace the object (after the verb or preposition).
4. **Possessive adjectives** go before a noun (my, your, his...).
5. **Possessive pronouns** replace a noun (mine, yours, his...).
6. **Reflexive pronouns** refer back to the subject (myself, yourself...).
7. Match the pronoun with the noun in **number** (singular/plural).
8. There are **no apostrophes** in possessive pronouns (yours, hers, ours, theirs).
9. **They/them/their** can be used for unknown gender or nonbinary people.
10. **It** is used for things, animals (not pets), weather, and time.
11. Use **'s** for singular possessives: *Lisa's essay*.
12. Use **'** for plural nouns ending in **-s**: *the girls' books*.
13. Use **'s** for plural nouns NOT ending in **-s**: *children's shoes*.
14. **Possessive pronouns NEVER take an apostrophe**: *its, whose, ours, yours, hers, theirs*.
15. **It's** = contraction of *it is* or *it has*; **its** = possessive.
16. **Who's** = contraction of *who is*; **whose** = possessive.
